It starts by itself. You do nothing.

  1. You pick up

    No button, no app to open. When the call plays on your Mac, Tell starts listening.

  2. It listens to the caller

    Only the caller’s side, on your own device. It never speaks to the caller and keeps no recording.

  3. You see two answers

    One calm card that updates as the call goes on, with the words and sounds behind it. It can change its mind.

Two questions, answered separately.

A scammer can be a real person. A harmless reminder can be an AI. So Tell says what the voice sounds like and what the call is asking for, as two answers, each with its evidence.

It updates live and can change its mind.

It does not wait for the call to end. Early on it says it is still listening. If the caller later asks you to pay, keep a secret or act right now, the card changes and says when. A red banner appears only for a corroborated scam pattern, and you can dismiss it.

Where it fits.

Services such as Hiya and Google also detect scams in real time or on the device. Tell does not claim to be the only one. What it shows is different: the voice and the words as two separate answers, with the evidence, starting by itself, on your own device. We evaluate and fine-tune open-source models, and we do not publish an accuracy figure because our test set is still small.

Questions, answered plainly.

What can it not hear?

Anything that is not played through the device it runs on. It does not hear a call held to your ear on a phone. Phone lines and speakers strip detail from a voice, so on a bad line the voice check can be unsure. It then says so and leans on the words.

Can it be wrong?

Yes. The scores are scores, not probabilities. A real person can sound scripted, and a good fake can sound natural. Not sure yet is a real answer, and you should still hang up and call back on a number you trust if money or codes come up.

Does it record my calls?

No audio is saved. It analyses the caller’s audio in memory and clears it when the call ends. A history of flagged calls is off unless you turn it on, and holds only the time, the verdict and the top reasons.

Do I need to tell the other person?

Rules about listening to calls vary by state and country. If yours requires telling the other person, tell them. We are not a lawyer, so check the rules where you live.

Does it speak to the caller?

Never. It only shows you what it heard.

What does it run on?

Open-source models on your own machine: speech to text, voice checks and a small local language model for scam wording. Our own rules combine them, and show you why.
